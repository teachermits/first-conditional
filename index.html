How do we structure the First Conditional?

We use the First Conditional to talk about real and possible situations in the future. Every First Conditional sentence connects two ideas: a Condition (the cause) and a Result (what happens next).

PART A

The Condition (IF-Clause)

Shows the possible future action or situation.

If + Subject + Present Simple

Example: "If it rains..." / "If you don't study..."

PART B

The Result (Main Clause)

Shows the future consequence of that condition.

Subject + will / won't + Base Verb

Example: "...I will stay home." / "...you won't pass."

Interactive Analysis: Click an example to inspect it!

Notice the verb tenses and where the comma ( , ) goes.

Sentence Structure Breakdown

💡 3 Golden Rules to Remember

  • 1. Never put "will" after "if"! Say "If it rains" (NOT If it will rain).
  • 2. Use a comma ( , ) when the If-clause comes first: "If I study, I will pass."
  • 3. No comma when the Result comes first: "I will pass if I study."
